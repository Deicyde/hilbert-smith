# Symmetric forms under addition

Let $C$ be an additive category with a duality $(D, \varpi)$ as in
[Transpose involution and orthogonal sums](transpose-involution.md).
Symmetric forms are closed under addition: the zero form is
symmetric, and the sum of two symmetric forms is symmetric. When
the transpose also preserves negation, negations of symmetric
forms are symmetric, so symmetric forms are an additive subgroup.

Cited wherever forms are added or subtracted: the Witt monoid
sums symmetric forms, and the doubling map adds a form to itself.

*Proof sketch.* The transpose of zero is zero, and the transpose
of a sum is the sum of the transposes, so symmetry of the
summands gives symmetry of the sum. Negation is the same from
preservation of negation by the transpose.
