# Block duality

Let $Q$ be a [block](block-pair.md) whose meshes are at most $h$, with every cell and its dual labelled at the cell's centre in the product of the factor realizations, and $d$ the differential of each complex (on dual complexes $d_r=(-1)^rd^*$). Then:
1. $T\varphi_Q=\varphi_Q$, $T\varphi_{\partial Q}=\varphi_{\partial Q}$, $\partial\varphi_{\partial Q}=0$ and $\partial\varphi_Q=j_\%\varphi_{\partial Q}$;
2. $\theta_Q$ and $b_Q$ are chain maps, and with $A_j=\theta_{F_j}b_{F_j}$, $A'_j=b_{F_j}\theta_{F_j}$ (where $\theta_{F_j}$ is $\varphi^s$ for a circle, $\theta_I$ for an arc and $1$ for a point),
$$K_{Q,1}=\sum_j1\otimes\dots\otimes1\otimes K^{(j)}\otimes A_{j+1}\otimes\dots\otimes A_\ell\otimes1_P,\qquad K_{Q,2}=\Xi_Q^{-1}\Bigl(\sum_j1\otimes\dots\otimes1\otimes K'^{(j)}\otimes A'_{j+1}\otimes\dots\otimes A'_\ell\otimes1\Bigr)\Xi_Q$$
satisfy $\theta_Qb_Q-1=dK_{Q,1}+K_{Q,1}d$ and $b_Q\theta_Q-1=dK_{Q,2}+K_{Q,2}d$. Here $(K^{(j)},K'^{(j)})$ is $(K,K')$ of circle duality for a circle, $(K_1,K_2)$ of the arc pair for an arc and $(0,0)$ for a point, and tensors of maps carry the sign $(f\otimes g)(x\otimes y)=(-1)^{|g||x|}f(x)\otimes g(y)$;
3. $\varphi_Q,\varphi_{\partial Q},\theta_Q,b_Q,K_{Q,1},K_{Q,2}$ have $d_\infty$-propagation at most $h$;
4. $dz_Q=j\,z_{\partial Q}$ with $z_{\partial Q}=\sum_{F_j\text{ arc}}(-1)^{a_j}\kappa_j(z_{F_1}\otimes\dots\otimes([m_j]-[0])\otimes\dots\otimes e_4)$, and $\varepsilon\theta_Q(\alpha)=\alpha(z_Q)$ for every $\alpha$ of degree $0$;
5. the block pair is a strict $N_Q$-dimensional symmetric Poincaré pair in finite-dimensional based $\mathbb Q$-modules; in particular $(\partial Q,\varphi_{\partial Q})$ is a strict $(N_Q-1)$-dimensional symmetric Poincaré complex;
6. let $(Q_i)$ be blocks with the same factor types and meshes at most $h_i\to0$, and let each cell $b$ of $Q_i$ (and its dual) carry a label $\lambda_i(b)$ in a compact metric space $X$ such that, for every $c>0$, $\sup\{d_X(\lambda_ib,\lambda_ib'):d_\infty(x_b,x_{b'})\le ch_i\}\to0$. Then $(j_i,\varphi_{Q_i},\varphi_{\partial Q_i})_i$ is a strict $N_Q$-dimensional symmetric Poincaré pair in $\mathcal A_{\mathbf 1}(X)$.

*Proof sketch.* (1) By the [Leibniz rule](../../l-theory/localization/products/product-leibniz.md), $\partial\varphi_Q=\sum_j(-1)^{a_j}\varphi_{F_1}\otimes\dots\otimes\partial\varphi_{F_j}\otimes\dots\otimes\varphi_P$. Only arc factors contribute, with $\partial\varphi_I=j_\%\varphi_C$ ([arc duality](arc-duality.md) (1)), and [pushforward of products](../../l-theory/localization/products/product-push.md) turns the $j$-th term into $(j\kappa_j)_\%\varphi_{Q_j}$. So $\partial\varphi_Q=j_\%\varphi_{\partial Q}$. Then $j_\%\partial\varphi_{\partial Q}=\partial\partial\varphi_Q=0$, and $j_\%$ is injective because $j$ is injective and $j^*$ surjective. [Products commute with T](../../l-theory/localization/products/product-involution.md), and $T$ commutes with pushforward. (2) Relative cochains of a product are the product of the relative cochains, so by the [duality map of a product](../../l-theory/localization/products/product-duality-map.md) applied blockwise, $\theta_Q=(\theta_{F_1}\otimes\dots\otimes\theta_{F_\ell}\otimes\varphi_P)\circ\Xi_Q$. Telescope as in the [Poincaré theorem](../grid/chain-model-poincare.md), using circle duality (3) and arc duality (2). (3) Each factor moves only its own coordinate, by at most its mesh ([circle duality](../grid/circle-duality.md) (5), arc duality (4)). (4) The Leibniz rule for chains, and arc duality (5) with circle duality (4).

(5) By (1), it remains to show that the pair map and $(\varphi_{\partial Q})_\natural$ are chain homotopy equivalences. $j$ is degreewise split injective with quotient $\pi$, and $\theta_Q$ is an equivalence by (2), so the [split pair criterion](../../l-theory/base/pairs/split-pair-criterion.md) gives the pair map. For the boundary, write $g=\pi\varphi_Q:Q^{N_Q-*}\to Q/\partial Q$, a chain map that is an equivalence by step 5 of that criterion. Let $\mathcal C$ be the [mapping cone](../../l-theory/base/complexes/cone.md). Then:
- $p:\mathcal C(\pi^*)\to\partial Q^{N_Q-*}$, $(x,y)\mapsto j^*x$, and $q:\Sigma\partial Q\to\mathcal C(\pi)$, $y\mapsto(0,jy)$, are chain maps. Splitting $Q=\partial Q\oplus Q/\partial Q$ by the basis, as in step 3 of that criterion, makes each of them a [triangular](../../l-theory/base/complexes/triangular-map.md) map of [twisted sums](../../l-theory/base/complexes/twisted-sum.md) whose diagonal components are an identity and a map between the zero complex and the cone of an identity, which is contractible. So both are equivalences by [triangular equivalences](../../l-theory/base/complexes/triangular-equivalence.md);
- $M:\mathcal C(\pi^*)\to\mathcal C(\pi)$, $M(x,y)=(gx,\theta_Qy)$, is a chain map because $g\pi^*=\pi\theta_Q$; it is triangular with diagonal components $g$ and $\Sigma\theta_Q$, hence an equivalence ([shifts](../../l-theory/base/complexes/shift-equivalence.md));
- with $L(x,y)=(0,(-1)^r\varphi_Qx)$ on degree $r$, $dL+Ld=M+q\circ\Sigma(\varphi_{\partial Q})_\natural\circ\epsilon\circ p$, where $\epsilon_r=(-1)^r:\partial Q^{N_Q-*}\to\Sigma\,\partial Q^{N_Q-1-*}$ is an isomorphism of complexes. This uses $d\varphi_Q-\varphi_Qd=j\varphi_{\partial Q}j^*$ from (1).

So $q\circ\Sigma(\varphi_{\partial Q})_\natural\circ\epsilon\circ p\simeq-M$ is an equivalence, and by two-out-of-three $\Sigma(\varphi_{\partial Q})_\natural$ is one, hence so is $(\varphi_{\partial Q})_\natural$ (apply the inverse shift). The degree conditions hold since $Q$ lives in $[0,N_Q]$ and $\partial Q$ in $[0,N_Q-1]$.

(6) By (3) and the label hypothesis, every map in (1)-(2), the based inclusions and projections, and the differentials have propagation tending to $0$, so they are morphisms of the [asymptotic category](../../categories/asymptotic/asymptotic-category.md), and the identities of (1)-(2) hold there exactly. The argument of (5) uses only these identities and lemmas valid in any duality category, so it applies in $\mathcal A_{\mathbf 1}(X)$.
