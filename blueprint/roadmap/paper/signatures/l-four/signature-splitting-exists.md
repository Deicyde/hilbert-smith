# Invariant signature splittings exist

Let $W$ be a finite-dimensional real vector space, $b$ a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on $W$, and $\rho$ an action of a finite group $G$ on $W$ by $b$-isometries. Then there are $G$-invariant subspaces $V^+,V^-$ with $W=V^+\oplus V^-$, $b$ positive definite on $V^+$ and negative definite on $V^-$. Such a pair is called a signature splitting.

*Proof sketch.* Average any inner product over $G$ to get an invariant inner product $\langle\,,\rangle$. Let $B$ be the operator with $b(x,y)=\langle x,By\rangle$. $B$ is self-adjoint and commutes with $G$. $B$ is invertible because $b$ is nondegenerate. Take $V^\pm$ to be the sums of the eigenspaces of $B$ with positive and negative eigenvalues (spectral theorem).
