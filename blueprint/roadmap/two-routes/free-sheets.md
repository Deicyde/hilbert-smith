# Free sheets, and putting the test over them

*Where does the action carry a free cyclic symmetry that a coordinate can record, and how does the test come to lie over it?*

Both contradictions come from a cyclic symmetry that permutes pieces of the invariant, and that symmetry is visible only where a finite quotient of the group acts freely.
This stage finds such a region, records the symmetry by a locally constant coordinate that shifts by a character, and arranges that the part of the test that matters lies over the region.
In the HS paper it is the end of Section 8, together with the sheet function in the proof of Lemma 4.1; in the preprint it is Lemmas 5.4 to 5.7 and Proposition 5.8.

## HS_proof

In the HS paper the free region falls out of the averaging.
Because the centre is free, a closed ball $\overline B(0,\rho_+)$ can be chosen to miss $\operatorname{Fix}(H)$, and $Z^+ = F_0^{-1}(\overline B(0,\rho_+))$ is compact, invariant, and disjoint from $\operatorname{Fix}(H)$, since $F_0$ is the identity there ([HS paper, lines 797-803](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L797-L803)).
Which closed subgroups can stabilize a point of $Z^+$? Not $H = p^j\mathbb{Z}_p$ itself, since $Z^+$ misses $\operatorname{Fix}(H)$, and every other closed subgroup of $H$ lies in $H' = p^{j+1}\mathbb{Z}_p$.
So every stabilizer of a point of $Z^+$ lies in $H'$, the group $C_p = H/H'$ of order $p$ permutes the $H'$-orbits in $Z^+$ without fixed points, and passing to $C_p$-orbits is a $p$-sheeted covering: a small enough neighbourhood has $p$ pairwise disjoint translates ([HS paper, lines 818-821](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L818-L821)).

Two objects come out of this covering.
The first is a detector, a continuous coordinate on $Z^+$ that rotates by a character: a map $\widetilde\rho: Z^+ \to T = S^{2N-1} \subset \mathbb{C}^N$ (the paper writes $m$ for $N$) with $\widetilde\rho(hx) = \chi(h)\widetilde\rho(x)$, where $\chi$ is the character $H \to H/H' = C_p \subset S^1$ and $C_p$ acts on $T$ by scalars, freely and isometrically.
It is glued from the covering: on each of $m$ trivializing sets the sheet index gives a $p$-th root of unity, and these $m$ local coordinates, damped by the square roots of a partition of unity so that the vector they form has length one, make up the map to the unit sphere of $\mathbb{C}^m$ ([HS paper, lines 821-830](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L821-L830)).
$T$ becomes a control space of the invariant.
The second lives on $T$: over a piece of a trivializing cover of $T/C_p$ there is a locally constant sheet function $c$ with values in $C_p$ and $c(hx) = \bar h\,c(x)$ (proof of Lemma 4.1, [HS paper, lines 335-337](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L335-L337)), and $c$ is what identifies tensoring with the permutation form $\tau$ (the forms $\tau_i = \mathbb{Q}[G_i/P_i]$ of the finite quotients $G_i$ chosen below) with $p$ copies of the identity there ([HS paper, lines 339-347](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L339-L347)).
In the paper $c$ stays on $T$, where it untwists $\tau \otimes (-)$; it plays the part that the preprint's $\beta$ plays. Its composite with the detector, a locally constant function on part of $Z^+$ that shifts by the character, is this chapter's comparison with $\beta$, not an object of the HS paper.
The cover of $T/C_p$ is fixed once, independently of the index $i$ of the finite quotients ([HS paper, lines 350-351](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L350-L351)).

The test already lies where the detector is defined.
Put $y = s_\rho(0)$, antipodal to $\infty$ for a round metric, and fix buffers $r < R < t < u$ below $d(y,\infty)$.
The part of the sphere where $f$ is not constant then pulls back into the interior of $Z^+$: each preimage $\mathcal K_v = f^{-1}(\overline B(y,v))$ with $v < d(y,\infty)$ is a compact invariant subset of it. The paper also fixes the exterior $Z_0 = \{z : d(y,z) \ge R\}$ that the later quotients discard ([HS paper, lines 831-838](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L831-L838)).
So the test never moves.
What grows instead is the finite quotient: only after $T$, the detector and the buffers are fixed does the paper choose $K_i = p^{\ell_i}\mathbb{Z}_p \subset H'$ with $\ell_i \to \infty$, $G_i = H/K_i$ and $P_i = H'/K_i$ ([HS paper, lines 839-846](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L839-L846)).
Why the whole sequence is needed is explained in [The group that carries the invariant](the-invariant.md).

## OpenAI's proof

In the preprint the free region depends on the order.
For $k \ge 1$, $m = p^k$ and $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/m}$, Lemma 5.6 finds a point of $W$ with stabilizer inside $p^kG$, since otherwise every stabilizer would contain $p^{k-1}G$, against faithfulness.
On $Z = W/p^kG$ the group $G/p^kG$ then acts freely at the image of that point; intersecting translates of disjoint neighbourhoods of its $m$ orbit points gives an open $N$ with disjoint translates, and $V$, the image of $N$ in $Y \setminus \{y_\infty\}$, is covered by $m$ sheets.
The value $\zeta^j$ on the $j$-th sheet defines a locally constant $\beta$ on $q^{-1}(V)$ with $\beta(gw) = \zeta^{\bar g}\beta(w)$ ([preprint, Lemma 5.6](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L386-L434)).
The test $a$ stays fixed while $k$ and $V$ vary ([preprint, §5, lines 381-384](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L381-L384)).

Since $V$ is a small region that depends on $k$, the test has to be moved into it.
For each nonempty open $V \subset Y \setminus \{y_\infty\}$ with complement $F$, Proposition 5.8 gives $s > 0$, a degree-$s$ map $D_s$ of $S^d$, and $h: Y \to S^d$ homotopic to $D_s a$ and constant on $F$, so the source of the nonconstant part of $h$ lies in $q^{-1}(V) \subset W$ ([preprint, Proposition 5.8](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L478-L517)).
The proof has three ingredients.

- Rational triviality on $F$. Sliding the pinch along a path in connected $W$ into a ball inside $q^{-1}(V)$ makes $aq$ homotopic to a map constant on $q^{-1}(F)$, so the class $(a\vert_F)^*\gamma_d$ dies after pulling back to $q^{-1}(F)$. Lemma 5.4 says pullback along an orbit map is injective on rational Čech cohomology: orbits are compact and zero-dimensional, so proper base change gives $Rr_* = r_*$, and averaging locally constant rational functions over $G$ splits the unit ([preprint, Lemma 5.4](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L209-L275)). Hence $(a\vert_F)^*\gamma_d = 0$ ([§5, lines 491-511](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L491-L511)).
- Odd spheres. A rationally trivial map from a compact metrizable space to $S^d$, $d \ge 3$ odd, becomes nullhomotopic after composing with some $D_s$: approximate through a finite nerve, then use that the telescope of degree maps is a $K(\mathbb{Q}, d)$, by Serre's finiteness of the higher homotopy groups of $S^d$ and his result on degree maps ([preprint, Lemma 5.5](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L287-L377)).
- Extension. A homotopy on a closed subset of a compact metrizable space extends, by Tietze and normalization ([preprint, Lemma 5.7](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L440-L471)); the endpoint of the extended nullhomotopy of $D_s a\vert_F$ is $h$.

Remark 5.9 records the outcome: the source of the moved test away from its base value is an open subset of $W$ over the sheets of Lemma 5.6, with its orientation and action, and $s$ may depend on $V$ and $k$ ([preprint, §5, lines 519-537](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L519-L537)).

## What is shared

- Freeness comes from the classification of closed subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ ([HS paper, lines 818-819](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L818-L819); [preprint, §5, lines 409-413](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L409-L413)).
- The coordinate: HS_proof's sheet function $c$ on $T$ and OpenAI's $\beta$ on $q^{-1}(V)$ are both locally constant functions to a cyclic group that shift by the character, built from a small neighbourhood with disjoint translates ([HS paper, lines 335-337](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L335-L337); [preprint, §5, lines 418-433](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L418-L433)).
- Both need the nonconstant part of the test over the region where the coordinate is defined ([HS paper, lines 837-838](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L837-L838); [preprint, §5, lines 487-488](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L487-L488)).

## What differs

- Size of the symmetry. HS_proof's has order $p$ and covers the compact set $Z^+$ where the test lives, so the test never moves. OpenAI's has order $p^k$, with $k$ chosen so that $p^k > 4L_d$, on a small region $V$ that depends on $k$, so the test must be moved ([preprint, §6, lines 464-466](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L464-L466)).
- What grows. HS_proof keeps the divisor $p$ and lets the finite quotients $G_i$ grow, because their representatives act only approximately. OpenAI's proof uses the exact action of $G$ on the orbit sheaf ([preprint, §6, lines 25-35](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L25-L35)) and lets the character order $p^k$ grow.
- The detector. HS_proof's continuous $\widetilde\rho$ into a fixed free sphere $T$, which becomes a control space, has no counterpart in OpenAI's proof. There $\beta$ is only a coefficient isometry on the eigensheaves ([preprint, §6, lines 421-435](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L421-L435)).
- Moving the test is where OpenAI's proof uses rational Čech cohomology and the homotopy groups of odd spheres, which the preprint uses nowhere else; HS_proof has nothing like it. Proper base change is not special to this step: Lemma 6.1 applies Lemma 5.4's identity $Rq_* = q_*$ and uses proper base change again ([preprint, §6, lines 54-58](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L54-L58); [lines 92-94](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L92-L94)).
