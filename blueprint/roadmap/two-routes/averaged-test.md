# An invariant degree-one map to a sphere

*How does the action produce an invariant degree-one map to a sphere, the test that the invariant will read?*

Once the action lives in one chart, both proofs turn it into a single map to a sphere that the small subgroup cannot see and that has degree one, so that the invariant has something nonzero to read.
In the HS paper this is the rest of the geometric data of Section 8, with the stabilization by $\mathbb{CP}^2$ at the start of Section 9; in the preprint it is the stabilization to an odd dimension $d$ and Lemmas 5.2 and 5.3.
The averaging device is the same; what the proofs do with the resulting map is not.

## HS_proof

The HS paper averages points.
On the invariant set $\Omega$ in the chart, with Haar probability measure $\mu$ on $H$, the map $F_0(x) = \int_H hx\,d\mu(h)$ takes each point to the barycentre of its $H$-orbit in coordinates ([HS paper, lines 787-792](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L787-L792)).
It is continuous and $H$-invariant, moves no point by more than $2D$, and is the identity on $\operatorname{Fix}(H)$, since a fixed point is its own barycentre ([HS paper, lines 793-796](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L793-L796)).
A pinch $s_\rho$ sends the ball $B(0,\rho)$ onto the sphere minus $\infty$ by an orientation-preserving homeomorphism and everything else to $\infty$, and the test is $f = s_\rho F_0$ on $\Omega$, $f = \infty$ elsewhere ([HS paper, lines 804-809](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L804-L809)).
Because $F_0$ moves points so little, $f$ is constantly $\infty$ away from the ball $B(0,\rho + 2D)$, so it makes sense on the one-point compactification $S^n$ of the coordinate space.
Composing the straight-line homotopy from $F_0$ to the identity with the pinch gives a homotopy of maps of $S^n$ from $f$ to $s_\rho$, equal to $\infty$ outside one fixed compact set at every time; $s_\rho$ has degree one, so $f$ does too, and so $f$ is onto, which makes the set $Z^+$ of points that $F_0$ sends into a small closed ball about the centre nonempty ([HS paper, lines 810-816](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L810-L816)).

The chain models then live on $S^n \times \mathbb{CP}^2$, of dimension $n + 4$, with the sphere factor the compactified coordinate space and the control maps ignoring $\mathbb{CP}^2$ ([HS paper, lines 850-858](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L850-L858)).
The end of the proof reads the $\mathbb{CP}^2$ factor: after the $n$ boundary maps only a copy of $\mathbb{CP}^2$, of signature $1$, is left, with a fixed sign ([HS paper, lines 117-119](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L117-L119); Proposition 12.4, [lines 1289-1298](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1289-L1298)); [Why the class is not zero](normalization.md) has the computation.

HS_proof keeps using the map $f$ itself, not only its homotopy class.
Each completed map satisfies $f\widehat h = f$ ([HS paper, lines 887-891](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L887-L891)); the graph type of a matrix is defined through propagation measured by $f$ and through the detector on the compact sets $f^{-1}(\overline{B}(y,v))$ ([HS paper, lines 900-906](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L900-L906)); the compression cuts along $f^{-1}(\overline{B}(y,t))$ ([HS paper, lines 984-986](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L984-L986)); and the scalar complex is labelled by $f$ ([HS paper, lines 1096-1099](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1096-L1099)).
Only the boundary evaluation trades $f$ for $s_\rho$, through the fixed homotopy above ([HS paper, lines 1300-1304](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1300-L1304)).

## OpenAI's proof

The preprint first stabilizes.
It fixes an odd $d > n$ with $d \ge 3$, puts $W = O \times \mathbb{R}^{d-n}$ with the product orientation and the trivial action on the new coordinates, and takes $S^d = \mathbb{R}^d \cup \{\infty\}$ as the compact test source. Its orientation form represents the orientation class $u_d \in E_d(S^d)$, the class of the constant sheaf with its orientation pairing in the Witt group of [the invariant](the-invariant.md), whose rational image spans the line $E_d(S^d) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ (see [Reading an integer](integrality.md); [preprint, §5, lines 75-86](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L75-L86)).
The collapse $c: S^d \to W^+$ sends everything outside $W$ to $\infty$; it is continuous, the action extends to $W^+$ fixing $\infty$, and $W^+$ and its orbit spaces are compact metrizable ([preprint, Lemma 5.2](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L88-L133)).

Lemma 5.3 averages maps rather than points ([preprint, Lemma 5.3](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L135-L183)).
It starts from a degree-one pinch $P_{z,r}: W^+ \to S^d$, a rescaled copy of a fixed map that collapses the outside of the unit ball to a point $\xi$, on a ball $\overline B(z,r) \subset W$.
With $S^d$ the unit sphere of $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$, a smaller open subgroup $G' \subset G$ moves every value of $P_{z,r}$ by less than $1/2$, so the vector-valued Haar average $A$ of $w \mapsto P_{z,r}(hw)$ over $h \in G'$ stays within $1/2$ of $P_{z,r}$.
No segment from $P_{z,r}(w)$ to $A(w)$ meets $0$, and normalizing the segments is a homotopy, fixed at $\infty$ and equal to $\xi$ off a compact subset of $W$, from $P_{z,r}$ to the invariant map $A/\lVert A\rVert$ ([preprint, §5, lines 163-179](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L163-L179)).
Once $G'$ replaces $G$, write $q: W^+ \to Y = W^+/G$ for the orbit map and $y_\infty = q(\infty)$; the invariant map descends to $a: Y \to S^d$ with $\deg(aqc) = 1$ ([preprint, §5, lines 180-182](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L180-L182)).

The test that Section 6 reads is a composite $f_t = tqc: S^d \to X$ for a map $t: Y \to X$ to a compact test space; here $t = a$ and $X = S^d$ ([preprint, §6, lines 101-112](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L101-L112)).
The invariant $E_d(X)$ lives over the test space $X$.
The orbit space $Y$ is only the domain of $t$, enters through ordinary direct images, and carries no duality ([preprint, §1, lines 302-304](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex#L302-L304); [§5, lines 532-534](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L532-L534); [§6, lines 131-134](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L131-L134)).
The classes read from $t$ depend only on its homotopy class ([preprint, Proposition 6.6](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L345-L348)), a freedom used when a positive-degree multiple of $a$ is replaced by a homotopic map (see [Free sheets](free-sheets.md); [preprint, §6, lines 470-476](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L470-L476)).

Odd $d$ is chosen for later stages, where its uses are explained: it makes the point group $E_d(\mathrm{pt})$ vanish ([preprint, Lemma 4.1](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L20-L33)), hence the rational sphere group a line where a lattice can be fixed ([Reading an integer](integrality.md); [preprint, Proposition 4.5](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L203-L214)) and localization away from a contractible set an integral isomorphism ([The group that carries the invariant](the-invariant.md); [§4, lines 504-515](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L504-L515)), and it is what Serre's odd-sphere argument needs in Lemma 5.5 ([Free sheets](free-sheets.md); [§5, lines 330-357](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L330-L357)).

## Side by side

|  | HS_proof | OpenAI's proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idea | Average chart coordinates over $H$, then pinch a small ball onto $S^n$: $f = s_\rho F_0$ | Pinch a ball onto $S^d$, average the sphere-valued map over a small open subgroup in $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$, normalize, descend to $a: Y \to S^d$ |
| Inputs | Haar measure on $H$; the straight-line homotopy; degree of sphere maps; the factor $\mathbb{CP}^2$ | Haar measure; Lemma 5.2 (one-point compactification, invariant metric); stabilization to odd $d > n$ |

## What is shared

- The device: Haar averaging over a small open subgroup, an average close to the unaveraged map, and a straight-line homotopy whose support stays in a compact part of the chart, so that degree one survives ([HS paper, lines 787-816](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L787-L816); [preprint, §5, lines 163-182](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L163-L182)).
- The source: both read the test on the compactified coordinate sphere, $S^n$ in HS_proof and $S^d$ through the collapse $c$ in OpenAI's proof ([HS paper, lines 850-851](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L850-L851); [preprint, §1, lines 253-254](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex#L253-L254)), so neither needs a global triangulation or orientation of $M$.
- Both later discard a neighbourhood of the image of $\infty$: in HS_proof the exterior $\{z : d(y,z) \ge R\}$, which contains $\infty$ ([HS paper, lines 831-835](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L831-L835)), in OpenAI's proof a contractible neighbourhood $U$ of $b_t = t(y_\infty)$ ([preprint, §6, lines 210-215](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L210-L215)).

## What differs

- The order of operations. HS_proof averages points, which makes sense because $\Omega$ sits in a coordinate ball, and pinches afterwards. OpenAI's proof pinches first, averages sphere-valued maps inside $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$, and normalizes.
- Where the test lives and what is used of it. HS_proof's $f$ is an invariant map from $\Omega \subset M$, and its exact level sets organize the chain models; only the boundary evaluation uses its homotopy class. OpenAI's $a$ is a map on the orbit space $Y$, the invariant it feeds lives over the target $S^d$, and the classes read from it depend only on its homotopy class.
- What the stabilization buys. HS_proof keeps the sphere in dimension $n$ and adds $\mathbb{CP}^2$, the source of the final signature $1$. OpenAI's proof raises the dimension to an odd $d$, which makes the rational sphere group a line, the localization $\ell_U$ an isomorphism, and Serre's argument available; the introduction calls this the only dimensional choice ([preprint, §1, lines 307-308](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex#L307-L308)).
