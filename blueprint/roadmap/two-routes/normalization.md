# Why the class is not zero

*What fixes the size of the class made from the action, so that divisibility by $p$, or $m$ equal parts, becomes impossible?*

In HS_proof this is Section 12 and the first half of Proposition 13.1: the class built from the action is cut down by $n$ localization boundaries, and its scalar image is shown to be one copy of $\mathbb{CP}^2$, of signature $1$.
In OpenAI's proof it is the last paragraph of the proof of Theorem 6.8: the total form of the averaged test has rational class $2\overline{u}_d$, so the $m$ character classes sum to $4\overline{u}_d$.
Both come down to the degree-one map of [An invariant degree-one map to a sphere](averaged-test.md) and to an orientation class whose signature is computed locally.

## HS_proof

The class to normalize is the normalized corner $\mathfrak{d} \in L_{n+4}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathbf{G},Z}(T \times S^n))$, over $T \times S^n$ modulo the exterior $Z = T \times Z_0$ ([HS paper, lines 1128-1137](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1128-L1137), [lines 831-836](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L831-L836)).
Let $U$ forget the group and $T$ but keep the sphere coordinate.
The group maps are uniformly small in that coordinate, so Lemma 5.2 and Proposition 10.2 identify $U_*\mathfrak{d}$ with $\mathfrak{m}_f$, the scalar class of the triangulated manifold $S^n \times \mathbb{CP}^2$ labelled through $f$ ([HS paper, lines 1139-1146](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1139-L1146); [lines 1035-1044](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1035-L1044); [line 850](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L850)).

The iterated boundary brings the degree down from $n+4$ to $4$ ([HS paper, lines 1148-1172](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1148-L1172)).
Each cut lowers the $L$-degree by one and the dimension of the sphere in the support by one.
The first cut is along the sphere $S_r(y)$ about $y$, which leaves the exterior on its outer side, so the exterior boundary of Proposition 6.1 applies ([HS paper, lines 522-529](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L522-L529)); each later cut splits the current sphere along an equator, and after $n$ of them the support is $T$ times the two points of $S^0$, of which one copy of $T$ is kept ([lines 1151-1159](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1151-L1159)).
The result is $\Delta_T$ from $L_{n+4}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathbf{G},Z}(T \times S^n))$ to $L_4(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{G}}(T))$, whose target is still over $T$ and still equivariant ([lines 1160-1163](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1160-L1163)).
Run on the scalar side, with neither $T$ nor a group, the same cuts give $\Delta_{\mathbf{1}}$, landing in $L_4(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbf{1}})$, where objects are sequences of rational vector spaces without labels and finitely supported sequences count as zero; and the two commute with forgetting, $U_*\Delta_T = \Delta_{\mathbf{1}}U_*$ ([lines 1164-1170](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1164-L1170); [lines 271-277](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L271-L277)).
None of these choices depends on $i$ ([lines 1164-1165](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1164-L1165)).

Proposition 12.4 evaluates the scalar side: $\Delta_{\mathbf{1}}\mathfrak{m}_f = \varepsilon\,[\mathbb{CP}^2]$, with a sign $\varepsilon = \pm 1$ set by the orientation and component choices, and $\sigma_{\mathrm{tail}}$ of this class is $\varepsilon$ in every coordinate ([HS paper, lines 1289-1297](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1289-L1297)).
Lemma 12.3 lets the labelling move along a homotopy: the same triangulations labelled by the two ends are cobordant through a triangulated prism whose relative duality is controlled by Lemma 12.1, and the classes agree in any fixed exterior quotient, whatever the homotopy does near the exterior ([HS paper, lines 1259-1287](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1259-L1287)).
The fixed homotopy from $f$ to the pinch $s_\rho$ ([lines 811-816](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L811-L816)) therefore gives $\mathfrak{m}_f = \mathfrak{m}_{s_\rho}$ ([lines 1300-1304](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1300-L1304)).
Since $s_\rho$ has degree one, Hopf's theorem that degree classifies maps $S^n \to S^n$ makes it homotopic to an orientation-preserving homeomorphism $g$ that carries a fixed disk $D$ and hemisphere flag onto the target ball and flag, and Lemma 12.3 applies again ([lines 1305-1310](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1305-L1310)).
For $g$, the triangulation splits into $D \times \mathbb{CP}^2$ and its complement, two controlled Poincaré pairs glued along $\partial D \times \mathbb{CP}^2$, away from the exterior (Lemmas 12.1 and 12.2, [lines 1311-1315](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1311-L1315)).
The union formula of Lemma 6.2 says that the boundary of such a union is the interface ([HS paper, lines 554-561](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L554-L561)), so the first boundary is $\partial D \times \mathbb{CP}^2$ and each later one is the next equator times $\mathbb{CP}^2$ ([lines 1316-1317](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1316-L1317)).
After $n$ boundaries one copy of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ remains, with a sign that does not depend on $i$; its middle form is $\langle 1 \rangle$ by the complex orientation, so its signature is $1$ ([lines 1318-1323](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1318-L1323)).
The paper says what the factor $\mathbb{CP}^2$ does, namely survive the $n$ boundaries and supply the signature $1$ that collides with divisibility ([lines 117-120](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L117-L120)), but not why that manifold.
A degree count suggests the reason (this chapter's reading, not the paper's): $S^n \times \mathbb{CP}^2$ has dimension $n + 4$, so $n$ boundaries land in degree $4$, where the tail signature is defined ([lines 309-313](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L309-L313)), and $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is a closed oriented $4$-manifold of signature $1$.

Proposition 13.1 carries the value back to $T$ ([HS paper, lines 1328-1350](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1328-L1350)).
Naturality gives $U_*\Delta_T\mathfrak{d} = \Delta_{\mathbf{1}}U_*\mathfrak{d} = \Delta_{\mathbf{1}}\mathfrak{m}_f = \varepsilon[\mathbb{CP}^2]$, and $\alpha = \varepsilon\Delta_T\mathfrak{d}$ is a class in $L_4(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{G}}(T))$ ([lines 1338-1346](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1338-L1346)).
Its signatures are those of its scalar image: forgetting equivariance goes through the coefficient-of-identity trace, which recovers the underlying rational form from a group-ring form ([HS paper, lines 232-238](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L232-L238)), and the signature character at the identity is the ordinary signature ([lines 255-256](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L255-L256)).
So $\sigma_{\mathrm{tail}}(\alpha)$ is the constant sequence $1$ ([lines 1347-1349](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1347-L1349)).

## OpenAI's proof

The size to fix is that of the total test form.
For a test $t$, $A_t = Rf_{t*}\mathbb{C}_{S^d}$ with $f_t = tqc$, and $\alpha_t$ is the pushforward of the orientation pairing of $S^d$ with coefficient form $B(z,w) = \operatorname{Re}(z\overline{w})$ on the real plane $\mathbb{C}$ ([preprint, §6, lines 108-117](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L108-L117); [lines 15-16](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L15-L16)).
For the averaged test, $f_a = aqc$ has degree one ([preprint, Lemma 5.3](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L135-L150)).

The form $B$ is the orthogonal sum of two positive real lines, so $[A_a, \alpha_a] = 2(aqc)_* u_d$, where $u_d \in E_d(S^d)$ is the orientation class, the class of the constant sheaf with its orientation pairing, whose rational image $\overline{u}_d$ spans the line $E_d(S^d) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ ([preprint, §6, lines 486-488](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L486-L488); [§4, lines 253-257](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L253-L257)).
A map of degree $s$ acts by $s$ on the rational line (Proposition 4.8), so the rational image of $[A_a, \alpha_a]$ is $2\overline{u}_d$ ([preprint, §6, lines 488-489](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L488-L489)).
Proposition 6.4 gives $\sum_i z_i(t) = 2[A_t, \alpha_t]$ for every finite partition: the $m$ untwisted terms contribute $m[A, \alpha]$, and an explicit involutive isometry of $A^{\oplus m}$ shows that the twisted forms add up to $(A,\alpha) \perp (A,-\alpha)^{\perp(m-1)}$, of class $(2-m)[A,\alpha]$ ([preprint, Proposition 6.4](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L252-L291)).
Hence $\sum_i \overline{z}_i(a) = 4\overline{u}_d$ ([preprint, §6, lines 490-492](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L490-L492)).
Of the two doublings, the first is the price of working over the real plane $\mathbb{C}$ and the second is produced by the involution $2e_i - 1$ in Definition 6.3 ([preprint, §1, lines 297-299](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex#L297-L299)).
The plane is what lets characters act by isometries ([preprint, §6, lines 160-170](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L160-L170); see [Turning the action into classes](classes-from-action.md)), and the total form pays for it by counting the sphere twice.

The size $4\overline{u}_d$ is fixed and nonzero.
Because $d$ is odd, $E_d(S^d) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ is a line ([preprint, Proposition 4.5](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L203-L251)); a signature on a small ball shows that the orientation class of the semialgebraic theory is integral and nontorsion ([preprint, Proposition 4.6](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L262-L280)); and Theorem 4.7 carries this to $E_d$, where $\overline{u}_d$ spans the line ([preprint, Theorem 4.7](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L370-L398)).
Proposition 4.8 is itself a local count: over a small ball in a top simplex the pushed pairing is diagonal, with one entry $\pm 1$ per sheet, and its signature is the local degree ([preprint, §4, lines 436-458](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L436-L458)).
None of this depends on the action, the prime or $k$ ([preprint, §4, lines 383-385](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L383-L385)).

## What is shared

- Both read the size off the degree-one map of the averaged-test stage and a homotopy to a standard map. HS_proof moves $f$ to $s_\rho$ and then to a homeomorphism ([HS paper, lines 1300-1310](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1300-L1310)); OpenAI uses $\deg(aqc) = 1$ through the action of degree ([preprint, §6, lines 486-489](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L486-L489)).
- Both get their integer from an orientation and a local signature. HS_proof's surviving $\mathbb{CP}^2$ has middle form $\langle 1 \rangle$ from its complex orientation, read at point control ([HS paper, lines 1320-1323](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1320-L1323)); OpenAI's $u_d$ is the orientation class of $S^d$, made integral and nonzero by a signature on a small ball ([preprint, Proposition 4.6](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L262-L280)).
- Both fix every sign and normalization before the group order varies ([HS paper, lines 1290-1291](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1290-L1291); [preprint, §4, lines 383-385](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L383-L385)); [The collision](contradiction.md) compares the two orders of choices.
- Both need homotopy invariance: HS_proof's Lemma 12.3, in an exterior quotient fixed in the target sphere ([HS paper, lines 1283-1284](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1283-L1284)), and the preprint's Theorem 2.9 inside Proposition 4.8 ([preprint, §4, lines 430-432](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L430-L432)). Homotopy invariance of OpenAI's classes, with its close-pair test, is on [Turning the action into classes](classes-from-action.md).

## What differs

- Degree and target. HS_proof descends from degree $n+4$ over $T \times S^n$ by $n$ boundaries, as above. OpenAI stays in degree $d$ on $S^d$ and computes no boundary.
- A bridge or none. HS_proof proves divisibility over $T$ and the normalization on the scalar side, and needs the forgetful functor $U$ and the identity-coefficient trace, as above, to join them. OpenAI's classes, their sum and $u_d$ all live in the one group $E_d(S^d)$.
- Degree theory. HS_proof uses Hopf's theorem that degree classifies sphere maps, to replace $s_\rho$ by a homeomorphism ([HS paper, lines 1305-1309](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1305-L1309)). OpenAI needs only that a degree-$s$ map acts by $s$ on the rational line, proved by counting sheets over a small ball ([preprint, Proposition 4.8](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L406-L485)).
- What comes out. HS_proof produces one class whose tail signature is exactly the constant sequence $1$ ([HS paper, lines 1333-1334](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1333-L1334)). OpenAI produces $m$ classes with known total $4\overline{u}_d$; each part is known only rationally, as $(4/m)\overline{u}_d$ ([preprint, §6, lines 490-495](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L490-L495)).
- The constant. HS_proof's $1$ is the signature of a closed $4$-manifold, read from the middle form of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ ([HS paper, lines 1318-1323](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1318-L1323)). OpenAI's $4$ is a product of two named factors of $2$, the real coefficient plane and the involution ([preprint, §1, lines 297-299](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex#L297-L299)).
