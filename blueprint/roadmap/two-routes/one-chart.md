# One chart and a small subgroup

*Where in the manifold does the argument take place, and which subgroup acts there?*

Both proofs start by shrinking the problem: a faithful action of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ on a connected manifold becomes the action of a small open subgroup $p^j\mathbb{Z}_p$ on an invariant open set inside one coordinate chart, and everything later happens in that chart or in a sphere built from it.
In the HS paper this is the opening of Section 8, right after the passage to a second-countable submanifold; in the preprint it is Proposition 5.1, the first step of Section 5.
The two proofs ask for different things at the base point, and they secure orientation in different ways.

## HS_proof

The HS paper first finds a point with trivial stabilizer (Lemma 8.1, [HS paper, lines 770-781](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L770-L781)).
The strategy is to show that if every point had a nontrivial stabilizer, the integers inside $\mathbb{Z}_p$ would have only finite orbits, and a theorem of Roberts on pointwise finite families of maps would then make some positive integer act as the identity ([HS paper, lines 776-777](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L776-L777); the reference is [lines 1477-1481](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1477-L1481)).
Finite orbits come from the shape of closed subgroups: a nonzero one is open in $\mathbb{Z}_p$, being the closure of the integer multiples of any of its elements of least valuation, so a point with nonzero stabilizer has a finite $\mathbb{Z}_p$-orbit.
And an integer $m > 0$ acting trivially is too much: the kernel is closed, so it would contain the closure of $m\mathbb{Z}$, the open subgroup $p^{v_p(m)}\mathbb{Z}_p$, which effectivity forbids.

Next comes a chart around the free point, chosen large enough to hold the closed ball of radius $4$.
Continuity of the action at the identity of $\mathbb{Z}_p$, together with compactness of the closed ball of radius $2$, then yields $j$ such that $H = p^j\mathbb{Z}_p$ displaces points of that ball by at most some $D < 1/64$; the $H$-saturation $\Omega$ of the open ball of radius $2$ is an invariant open set inside the chart ([HS paper, lines 782-788](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L782-L788)).
The paper does not say why it wants a free point. On this chapter's reading, what the free point buys is freedom in the order of choices: $j$ comes out of the continuity argument only after the point is fixed, and later a small closed ball about the centre must miss $\operatorname{Fix}(H)$ ([HS paper, lines 797-798](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L797-L798)).
A free point is fixed by no $p^j\mathbb{Z}_p$, whatever $j$ turns out to be.

Orientation is never imposed by passing to a subgroup, but it is used.
Each $h \in H$ is completed to a self-map $\widehat h(x) = x + \theta(x)(hx - x)$ of the coordinate sphere $S^n$, the one-point compactification of the chart's $\mathbb{R}^n$, with $\theta$ a cutoff equal to $1$ near the centre; shrinking $\theta$ to $0$ joins $\widehat h$ to the identity, so each $\widehat h$ has degree one ([HS paper, lines 880-886](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L880-L886)).
Small displacement is what makes this formula meaningful in one chart.
Degree one is why the chain approximations of the $\widehat h$ on fine triangulations of $S^n \times \mathbb{CP}^2$ (see [Turning the action into classes](classes-from-action.md)) fix the fundamental cycle ([HS paper, lines 907-913](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L907-L913)), which the paper then uses to match augmentations in the homotopies showing that the approximations preserve the duality, and to build the homotopies comparing them with their adjoints ([HS paper, lines 956-975](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L956-L975)).
Nothing outside this chart is used later, so no orientation or triangulation of the whole manifold is ever needed ([HS paper, lines 97-99](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L97-L99)).

## OpenAI's proof

The preprint asks less of its point: only that no open subgroup fix a whole neighbourhood of it ([preprint, §5, lines 31-50](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L31-L50)).
If there were no such point, some $F_j$, the interior of the fixed set of $p^j\mathbb{Z}_p$, would be nonempty.
It is also closed: near a limit point a smaller open subgroup fixes a neighbourhood, so $p^j\mathbb{Z}_p$ acts on a small connected saturated neighbourhood through a finite group, and Newman's theorem, in its fixed-open-set form (a periodic homeomorphism of a connected manifold that is the identity on a nonempty open set is the identity), makes it trivial there ([preprint, §5, lines 24-29](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L24-L29)).
Connectedness would give $F_j = M$, against faithfulness.

Around such a point $x$, take a coordinate ball $B$ and an open subgroup $H$ with $H\overline B$ in the chart and $Hx \subset B$.
Then $O = HB$ is open, invariant and connected, since each translate of $B$ meets $B$ ([preprint, §5, lines 52-61](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L52-L61)).
The kernel of $H$ on $O$ is closed; were it nonzero, it would be an open subgroup fixing the neighbourhood $O$ of $x$, which the choice of $x$ excludes ([preprint, §5, lines 61-66](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L61-L66)).
Last, the chart orients $O$; a homeomorphism of connected $O$ preserves or reverses that orientation, so a square preserves it, and $H$ is replaced by $G = 2H$, a change only for $p = 2$ ([preprint, §5, lines 68-72](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L68-L72)).

Orientation pays off in Section 6: it makes $G$ act by isometries of the pushed-forward duality pairing, so the generator $\gamma$ (the preprint's $T$) satisfies $\gamma^\dagger = \gamma^{-1}$ and the character projectors are self-adjoint ([preprint, Lemma 6.2, §6 lines 157-171](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L157-L171)).
The preprint never needs a free point.
For each $k$ it later gets, from faithfulness alone, a point whose stabilizer lies in $p^kG$ ([preprint, §5, lines 409-413](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L409-L413)).

## What is shared

- One chart and one small open subgroup, chosen by joint continuity and compactness of a closed ball, and a ball saturated by that subgroup to give a connected invariant open set in the chart ([HS paper, lines 782-788](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L782-L788); [preprint, §5, lines 52-61](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L52-L61)).
- The classification of closed subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ ([HS paper, lines 774-775](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L774-L775); [preprint, §5, lines 61-64](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L61-L64)).
- Orientation preservation is a requirement of both proofs, because the group must respect the duality read later. HS_proof needs the degree-one completed maps for its fundamental cycle and adjoint homotopies ([HS paper, lines 880-913](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L880-L913) and [968-975](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L968-L975)); OpenAI's proof needs orientation for the isometries of preprint Lemma 6.2 ([preprint, §5, lines 68-72](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L68-L72) and [§6, lines 160-170](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L160-L170)).
- The chart coordinates orient everything, so neither proof triangulates or orients $M$ beyond one chart ([HS paper, lines 97-99](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L97-L99); [preprint, §5, line 68](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L68)).

## What differs

- What is asked of the point. HS_proof wants a trivial stabilizer; on this chapter's reading, so that $H$, chosen after the point, cannot fix it. OpenAI's proof wants only that no open subgroup fix a neighbourhood, and finds points with stabilizer inside $p^kG$ later, one $k$ at a time.
- Orientation is met two ways. In HS_proof it is free: displacements are so small that each completed map is joined to the identity by shrinking the cutoff. OpenAI's proof uses the preserve-or-reverse dichotomy and passes to $2H$, of index two when $p = 2$.
- Faithfulness on the chart set. In HS_proof the free centre makes $H$ act faithfully on $\Omega$ with nothing to prove, and the argument stays on the manifold. OpenAI's proof argues faithfulness on $O$ because it then forgets $M$ and regards $O$ as an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ ([preprint, §5, lines 18-20](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L18-L20)); its Theorem 6.8 is about actions on connected open subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$ ([preprint, §6, lines 445-448](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L445-L448)).
- The rigidity theorem has a different shape: Roberts' is global (pointwise finite orbits force a finite image), Newman's is local (trivial on an open set forces trivial). HS_proof uses Newman's theorem later, in [the group step](groups-to-zp.md).
