# Why the symmetry forces divisibility

*Why does a free cyclic symmetry of $p$-power order force the classes made from the action to be divisible by $p$, or to split into equal parts?*

In HS_proof this is the algebraic half of the argument: Section 4 of the HS paper shows that every class over a fixed free $C_p$-space $T$ has signatures eventually divisible by $p$, before any action on a manifold appears.
In OpenAI's proof it is the preprint's Proposition 6.7 and the first half of the proof of its Theorem 6.8, applied to the character classes $z_i(a)$ of the averaged test $a$.
Both rest on one device: a locally constant coordinate on the free part that transforms by a character of $p$-power order, and so turns the symmetry into a relabelling.

## HS_proof

Here $T$ is compact and nonempty, $C_p$ acts on it freely, and its metric is invariant; the cyclic groups $G_i = C_{p^{a_i}}$ see $T$ only through their quotients $G_i/P_i = C_p$ ([HS paper, lines 154-156](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L154-L156), [lines 320-321](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L320-L321)).
Let $\tau_i = \mathbb{Q}[G_i/P_i]$ be the permutation module with its orthonormal form.
The functor $\tau\otimes(-)$ lets $G_i$ act on both factors and gives each new basis vector the label of the old one; it preserves duality, and $\nu = p\,\mathrm{id} - \tau\otimes(-)$ acts on the $L$-groups of $\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{G}}(T)$ ([HS paper, lines 322-327](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L322-L327)).
The operator $\nu$ compares $\tau\otimes(-)$ with $p$ copies of the identity.
For one free module the two are always isomorphic; the question is whether the isomorphism can be chosen naturally and without moving labels, and that is a question about the covering $T \to T/C_p$.

Locally it can (Lemma 4.1, [HS paper, lines 329-348](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L329-L348)).
Over a piece of $T$ on which the covering $T \to T/C_p$ is trivial there is a sheet function $c$ with values in $C_p$ and $c(hx) = \bar h\, c(x)$ ([lines 335-337](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L335-L337)).
Reading $c$ at the label of each basis vector tells which of the $p$ copies to put it in, and this re-indexing cancels the diagonal action ([lines 340-343](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L340-L343)).
It permutes basis vectors and keeps every label, so it is orthogonal and has propagation zero.
It is also natural: the sheets are compact and a fixed positive distance apart, and a morphism whose propagation tends to $0$ cannot, for large $i$, join a label on one sheet to a label on another; so the re-indexing identifies $\tau\otimes(-)$ isometrically with $p$ copies of the identity ([lines 344-347](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L344-L347)).
Hence $\nu$ kills every class supported over such a piece.

Globally $\nu$ is nilpotent (Lemma 4.2, [HS paper, lines 353-365](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L353-L365)).
The cover of $T/C_p$ by $\kappa$ compact pieces, each inside a trivialization, does not depend on $i$ ([lines 350-351](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L350-L351)).
The support Mayer–Vietoris sequence, from the support calculus of Lemma 2.2, does the rest: the boundary of $\nu x$ is computed on an overlap, where $\nu$ already vanishes, so $\nu x$ comes from the two sides, and induction on the number of pieces gives $\nu^\kappa = 0$ ([lines 356-364](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L356-L364)).

Characters turn nilpotence into divisibility (Theorem 4.3, [HS paper, lines 367-380](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L367-L380)).
Extract representing forms $(V_i, b_i)$ over $\mathbb{Q}[G_i]$ (Proposition 3.2); then $(p - \tau_i)^\kappa [V_i, b_i] = 0$ for all large $i$ ([lines 372-376](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L372-L376)).
Tensoring with a positive form multiplies signature characters ([lines 255-257](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L255-L257)), and the permutation character of $\tau_i$ is zero at a generator $g_i$, which fixes no point of $G_i/P_i$ ([line 377](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L377)).
So $p^\kappa$ times the signature character at $g_i$ is zero, and that character value vanishes ([line 378](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L378)).
Lemma 3.1 converts this into divisibility ([HS paper, lines 259-269](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L259-L269)).
Every character of $C_{p^a}$ is a power of one faithful character, so the signature character is an integer polynomial $F$ evaluated at that character; at the generator this value is a primitive $p^a$-th root of unity where $F$ vanishes, so the cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_{p^a}$ divides $F$, and at the identity $F(1)$ is the ordinary signature, which $\Phi_{p^a}(1) = p$ therefore divides.
The divisor is $p$ whatever the order $p^{a_i}$ is, and nothing in the argument uses geometry or homotopy invariance: it holds for every class in $L_4(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{G}}(T))$.

## OpenAI's proof

Fix $k \geq 1$, put $m = p^k$ and $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/m}$.
The characters of $G = \mathbb{Z}_p$ form the group $\Lambda$ of roots of unity of $p$-power order, and the orbit sheaf is the sum of its eigensheaves $\mathcal{H}_\lambda$ ([preprint, §6, lines 25-42](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L25-L42)).
Choose a transversal $D_0$ for the cosets of $\mu_m$ in $\Lambda$ and set $D_i = \zeta^i D_0$, so that multiplication by $\zeta$ permutes the $m$ parts cyclically ([preprint, §6, lines 396-402](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L396-L402)).
Lemma 5.6 supplies a nonempty open region $V \subset Y \setminus \{y_\infty\}$ over which $W/p^kG \to W/G$ is $m$ disjoint sheets, and a locally constant multiplier $\beta$ on $q^{-1}V$ with values in $\mu_m$ and $\beta(gx) = \chi(g)\beta(x)$, $\chi(1) = \zeta$ ([preprint, Lemma 5.6](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L386-L434); [§6, lines 403-409](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L403-L409)).

For a test concentrated over the sheets, the parts are equal ([preprint, Proposition 6.7](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L411-L443)).
If $h : Y \to S^d$ is constant, with value $b$, off $V$, then everything over $S^d \setminus \{b\}$ comes from $q^{-1}V$, where $\beta$ is defined ([§6, lines 422-424](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L422-L424)).
Multiplying the coefficient sheaf by $\beta$ is a real isometry because $\lvert\beta\rvert = 1$, and $\gamma M_\beta = M_{\zeta^{-1}} M_\beta \gamma$, where $\gamma$ is the action of $1 \in G$ (the preprint's $T$): it carries the $\lambda$-eigensheaf to the $\zeta^{-1}\lambda$-eigensheaf and so moves each part $D_i$ to $D_{i-1}$ ([§6, lines 424-432](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L424-L432)).
After pushforward it is an isometry of $(A_h, \alpha_h)$ away from $b$ that conjugates $e_i$ to $e_{i-1}$; the germ theorem carries it into the quotient category, where it makes the twisted forms for $e_i$ and $e_{i-1}$ isometric, so $z_i(h) = z_{i-1}(h)$ ([§6, lines 432-442](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L432-L442)).

The averaged test $a$ is not concentrated over $V$, so the proof of Theorem 6.8 moves it ([preprint, §6, lines 469-484](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L469-L484)).
Proposition 5.8 gives $s > 0$ and a map $h \simeq D_s a$ constant off $V$, where $D_s$ has degree $s$ ([preprint, Proposition 5.8](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L478-L489)).
Covariance (Proposition 6.5), homotopy invariance (Proposition 6.6) and Proposition 6.7 give $D_{s*} z_i(a) = z_i(D_s a) = z_i(h) = z_j(h) = D_{s*} z_j(a)$, and $D_{s*}$ is multiplication by $s$ on the line $E_d(S^d) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ ([preprint, Proposition 4.8](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-lattice.tex#L406-L418)).
So the rational images $\overline{z}_i(a)$ all agree; $s$ is cancelled only as a nonzero rational scalar, and the integral classes stay in the fixed lattice ([§6, lines 478-484](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L478-L484)).

Homotopy invariance of the classes needs its own argument, the close-pair test, because the neighbourhood that the classes discard moves with the base point ([preprint, Proposition 6.6](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L345-L392)); [Turning the action into classes](classes-from-action.md) gives it.

## Side by side

|  | HS_proof | OpenAI's proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idea | A sheet function untwists $\tau \otimes (-)$ over each trivialized piece of $T$, so $\nu = p - \tau$ vanishes locally and is nilpotent globally; signature characters at a generator then give $p \mid \sigma$ | The multiplier $\beta$ shifts every character part by $\zeta^{-1}$ over the sheet region, so the $m$ classes of a test concentrated there agree; covariance, homotopy and degree carry this to the averaged test |
| Inputs | Lemma 2.2 (support Mayer–Vietoris), Lemma 3.1, Proposition 3.2, Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2, Theorem 4.3 | Lemma 5.6, Proposition 5.8, Theorem 3.3 (germs), Propositions 6.5, 6.6 and 6.7, Theorem 2.9, Proposition 4.8 |

## What is shared

- The same mechanism: a locally constant coordinate with values in roots of unity, equivariant for the character, re-indexes the copies that the symmetry permutes. HS_proof's sheet function $c$ and OpenAI's multiplier $\beta$ are objects of this kind ([HS paper, lines 335-347](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L335-L347); [preprint, §6, lines 403-409](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L403-L409), [lines 424-435](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L424-L435)).
- Both untwisting maps are isometries for the same reason: they only permute basis vectors or multiply by unit scalars. HS_proof's re-indexing is orthogonal with propagation zero ([HS paper, lines 340-347](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L340-L347)); OpenAI's $M_\beta$ preserves the coefficient form $B(z,w) = \operatorname{Re}(z\overline{w})$ because $\lvert\beta\rvert = 1$ ([preprint, §6, lines 163-170](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L163-L170), [lines 425-426](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L425-L426)).
- Both end in the arithmetic of roots of unity of $p$-power order: HS_proof evaluates at a primitive $p^a$-th root and uses $\Phi_{p^a}(1) = p$ ([HS paper, lines 264-268](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L264-L268)); OpenAI splits $\Lambda$ into $m$ parts $D_i = \zeta^i D_0$ that multiplication by $\zeta$ permutes cyclically ([preprint, §6, lines 396-402](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L396-L402)).

## What differs

- Where the untwisting holds. HS_proof untwists over each piece of a finite cover of $T/C_p$ fixed before $i$ varies, and Mayer–Vietoris globalizes the result to every class over $T$ ([HS paper, lines 350-365](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L350-L365)). OpenAI untwists on one region $V$ that depends on $k$, and moves the test into $V$ instead of globalizing ([preprint, §5, lines 388-395](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/05-local-tests.tex#L388-L395)); see [Free sheets, and putting the test over them](free-sheets.md).
- What gets divided. HS_proof's character has order $p$, the group orders $p^{a_i}$ grow, and the conclusion is $p \mid \sigma$ with the divisor $p$ for every $i$. OpenAI's character has order $m = p^k$ and the conclusion is $m$ equal parts; the divisor grows with $k$ ([The collision](contradiction.md)).
- Homotopy. HS_proof's divisibility is algebra and holds for every class over $T$; it uses no homotopy invariance. OpenAI's equal-parts step needs covariance and homotopy invariance of the classes ([preprint, §6, lines 345-392](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/The-Hilbert-Smith-conjecture-in-every-finite-dimension-September-23-2026/build/sections/06-character-classes.tex#L345-L392)). HS_proof's nearest step, Lemma 12.3, serves its normalization instead ([HS paper, lines 1259-1287](https://github.com/adbrw/HS_proof/blob/5dee3407a627f35a2cdeb0a34ac9e0f1ee98f862/HSproof.tex#L1259-L1287); [Why the class is not zero](normalization.md)).
- Where the result lives. HS_proof's divisibility is a statement in $L_4(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{G}}(T))$, over $T$; its normalization is computed on the scalar side and joined to it later through forgetful functors ([Why the class is not zero](normalization.md)). OpenAI's equal parts and its normalization live in the one group $E_d(S^d)$.
