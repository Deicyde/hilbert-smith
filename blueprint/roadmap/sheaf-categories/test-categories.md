# Test categories

A *test space* is a locally compact, finite-dimensional, separable
metrizable space built from finite polyhedra: a finite polyhedron, a
space homeomorphic to one, an open subset of such, or a finite
product of these; points and the empty space are included. Write
$\mathsf D(X) = D^b(\mathrm{Sh}_\R(X))$ for the bounded derived
category of real sheaves.

$\Tzero(X)$ is the smallest full replete triangulated subcategory of
$\mathsf D(X)$ containing the proper direct images
$Rf_*\underline\R_B$ for every continuous $f : B \to X$ with $B$ a
compact finite polyhedron; $\T(X)$ is its closure under direct
summands. $\Pzero(X)$ and $\Pcat(X)$ are defined the same way with
$B$ a compact polyhedron in Euclidean space and $f$ continuous
semialgebraic in specified models. An allowed map for $\T$ is a
continuous map of test spaces; for $\Pcat$, a continuous
semialgebraic map (or restriction, or open inclusion) carrying
allowed generators to allowed generators.

*Well-definedness.* Every test space embeds as a locally closed
subset of some $\R^N$, so every allowed map has finite $c$-soft
dimension; the composition formula used is established before that
hypothesis is imposed, so the verification is not circular. The
generating maps are proper with bounded images, and the categories
are essentially small.
