# Symmetric cone

Let $(\#, \eta)$ be a triangulated duality with exactness sign
$\delta \in \{1,-1\}$ on a category in which multiplication by $2$
is invertible on every morphism group. Let $u : L \to \#L$ be
symmetric, with no invertibility assumption. In a triangle
$L \to \#L \to C \to L[1]$, the cone carries a nonsingular
symmetric form $\psi : C \to [1]\#C$ for the translated duality. It
is neutral and can be chosen to satisfy the cone-compatibility
identities $\psi v = -[1](\#w)$ and
$[1](\#v)\psi = \delta[1](\eta_L)w$. For $\# = \D_{X,r+1}$ this is a
neutral form of degree $r$.

*Proof sketch.* Dualize and rotate the triangle, complete to a
morphism of triangles, and average the third map with its signed
transpose (using invertibility of $2$) to impose symmetry for the
translated biduality. Two-out-of-three gives the isomorphism; the
construction is the symmetric boundary of $u$, hence neutral.
